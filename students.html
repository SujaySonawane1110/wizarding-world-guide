The youth of the four houses of Hogwarts, defining the future of the wizarding world.

THE NEXT GENERATION

From Gryffindor to Slytherin, Ravenclaw to Hufflepuff, the students of Hogwarts fill the ancient castle with life, rivalry, and magic. Whether competing on the Quidditch pitch, cramming in the library, or forming deep friendships, they carry the legacies of the founders.

CHARACTERS OF THE GROUP

OLIVER WOOD

Oliver Wood was the Gryffindor Quidditch Captain whose manically obsessive dedication to Quidditch strategy and team excellence defined his Hogwarts experience. His constant tactical speeches, often delivered at inconvenient times, stemmed from his genuine belief that Quidditch represented more than just a sport—it was an arena for demonstrating house excellence and personal mastery. His meticulous attention to strategy and his physical talent as a Keeper made him an accomplished athlete.

Wood's post-war career in professional Quidditch reflected his sustained passion for the sport. His transformation from student to professional athlete demonstrated how school excellence could translate to real-world success. His legacy at Hogwarts survived through his influence on younger players and his proven success in translating theory into practical competitive achievement.

HOUSE: Gryffindor ROLE: Quidditch Captain POSITION: Keeper

DRACO MALFOY

Draco Malfoy was Harry Potter's arch-rival and a primary antagonist throughout the series—a pale-faced, white-blond Slytherin raised in an atmosphere of pure-blood supremacy and Death Eater ideology. His bullying of Harry and other students stemmed from both genuine ideological belief and his family's expectations. His intellectual ability and magical skill were genuine, yet corrupted by his commitment to prejudicial ideology and his father's influence.

Malfoy's character arc culminated in his inability to ultimately commit to Voldemort's cause. When given the opportunity to capture Harry during the final battle, he hesitated, suggesting that his moral development had proceeded further than his public persona suggested. His survival and post-war life represented the possibility of redemption even for those raised in fascistic ideology, though such redemption would require genuine transformation and moral reckoning. His ultimate fate remained ambiguous, suggesting that even those who cracked under pressure might eventually find their humanity.

HOUSE: Slytherin WAND: 10", Hawthorn, Unicorn Hair PATRONUS: None

VINCENT CRABBE

Vincent Crabbe was one of Draco Malfoy's primary sidekicks, large, slow-witted, and aggressive. His role in Malfoy's bullying campaigns reflected both his loyalty to Malfoy and his own mean-spirited nature. His limited magical ability was compensated for by his physical size and his willingness to use violence. His participation in Death Eater activities demonstrated his ideological alignment with fascistic ideology.

Crabbe's death in the Room of Requirement, killed by his own uncontrolled Fiendfyre spell while attempting to destroy evidence of Death Eater activity, represented karmic justice through his own magical incompetence. His inability to manage the powerful destructive magic he summoned resulted in his death, removing one of Hogwarts' consistent bullies and Death Eater sympathizers. His demise symbolized the consequences of combining ideological commitment with magical inadequacy.

HOUSE: Slytherin WAND: Unknown FATE: Died in Room of Requirement

GREGORY GOYLE

Gregory Goyle was the other half of Draco Malfoy's bodyguard duo, large, lumpish, and mean-spirited in his approach to his peers. His limited intellect and magical ability did not prevent his enthusiastic participation in bullying campaigns. His commitment to Malfoy's ideology and his willingness to follow Malfoy's lead made him a reliable agent of oppression within Hogwarts.

Goyle's survival of the war, unlike his companion Crabbe, reflected his somewhat less aggressive approach to magical combat. His post-war fate remained largely unknown, suggesting his significance diminished once Malfoy's influence waned. His role throughout the series exemplified how bullying often required followers willing to abandon independent thought in favor of group conformity.

HOUSE: Slytherin WAND: Unknown ROLE: Beater (Slytherin)

PANSY PARKINSON

Pansy Parkinson was a Slytherin girl whose physical appearance (described in unflattering terms as "pug-faced") became the focus of widespread comment. Her mean-spirited nature and her enthusiastic participation in bullying campaigns made her one of Hogwarts' consistent aggressors. Her political opportunism and her attempt to personally hand Harry over to Voldemort in the final battle demonstrated her complete moral bankruptcy and her willingness to betray even her own peers for personal survival.

Parkinson's failure to deliver Harry to Voldemort resulted in her remaining at Hogwarts to face consequences from her peers. Her post-war fate involved social ostracism and professional limitations, representing societal consequences for her attempted betrayal. Her character illustrated how bullying behavior and ideological extremism could lead to permanent social exile even post-war.

HOUSE: Slytherin ROLE: Prefect LOYALTY: Malfoy / Voldemort

CORMAC MCLAGGEN

Cormac McLaggen was a large, arrogant Gryffindor student whose talented magical ability, particularly his skill as a Keeper, was undermined by his insufferable ego and his inability to cooperate effectively with teammates. His selection into the Slug Club despite his limited redeeming qualities reflected Horace Slughorn's sometimes questionable judgment. His aggressive competitiveness and his unwillingness to accept criticism made him a difficult teammate.

McLaggen's ultimate inability to secure a permanent position on the Gryffindor Quidditch team reflected his limitations as a team player. His magical talent was genuine, yet his personality defects prevented him from achieving success proportionate to his abilities. His post-war fate remained unknown, suggesting his significance was limited to his brief Hogwarts appearance.

HOUSE: Gryffindor CLUB: Slug Club POSITION: Keeper (Reserve)

CEDRIC DIGGORY

Cedric Diggory was the Hufflepuff Captain and Hogwarts Champion, a handsome young wizard whose genuine talent, fairness, and moral strength made him widely admired across all houses. His selection as Hogwarts' Triwizard Champion was richly deserved, reflecting his exceptional magical ability and his character. His relationships with both his Hufflepuff peers and other house members demonstrated his fundamental decency.

Cedric's tragic murder at Peter Pettigrew's hands during the Triwizard Tournament marked the first major casualty of Voldemort's renewed strength. His death served as Voldemort's confirmation of return to power, transforming the tournament from sporting competition to political statement. His loss represented the tragedy of potential unrealized—his many years of gifted magical study and character development ended by fascistic violence. His spirit's appearance during the final battle offered Harry crucial consolation and served as reminder of those lost to Voldemort's tyranny.

HOUSE: Hufflepuff WAND: 12¼", Ash, Unicorn Hair FATE: Deceased

FLEUR DELACOUR

Fleur Delacour was the Beauxbatons Academy Champion and a part-Veela student whose partial magical heritage gave her distinctive beauty and considerable charm magic. Her selection as Triwizard Champion reflected her significant magical abilities. Her initial seemingly superficial personality and her apparent focus on physical appearance masked deeper competence and genuine courage in facing tournament challenges.

Fleur's character development throughout the series revealed her to be more substantial than her initial presentation suggested. Her genuine care for others, particularly her romantic commitment to Bill Weasley and her loyalty to him after his scarring by Fenrir Greyback, demonstrated her fundamental decency and her commitment to relationships beyond physical attraction. Her marriage to Bill and her post-war life symbolized the connections forged across different wizarding communities through the shared experience of resistance to Voldemort.

SCHOOL: Beauxbatons WAND: 9½", Rosewood, Veela Hair ANCESTRY: Part-Veela

VIKTOR KRUM

Viktor Krum was the Durmstrang Institute Champion and a renowned international Quidditch star whose Seeker position fame preceded his appearance at Hogwarts. His grumpy demeanor on land contrasted sharply with his graceful flying ability in the air, where his magical talent and athletic prowess achieved full expression. His selection as Durmstrang Champion reflected his significant magical abilities and his athletic excellence.

Krum's interest in Hermione Granger and his demonstrated capacity for genuine emotion beneath his initially surly exterior suggested depths not immediately apparent. His eventual alignment with good forces and his survival of the war reflected his capacity for conscience and principled action despite his initial allegiance to a potentially compromised institution. His post-war career continued his professional Quidditch excellence, demonstrating how athletic talent could endure through political upheaval.

SCHOOL: Durmstrang WAND: 10¼", Hornbeam, Dragon Heartstring POSITION: Seeker

ROMILDA VANE

Romilda Vane was a younger Gryffindor student whose infatuation with Harry Potter initially manifested through somewhat inappropriate attempts to gain his romantic attention, including her attempted use of a love potion. Her initial immaturity and her focus on personal attraction reflected her youth. Her participation in DA training and her growing maturity over the series demonstrated her capacity for genuine development beyond her initial superficial presentation.

Vane's maturation over time, her willingness to join resistance activities, and her participation in the final battle showed her transformation from boy-obsessed younger student to committed fighter for justice. Her arc represented how youthful folly and immaturity were not necessarily permanent character traits but could give way to genuine growth and moral commitment. Her survival and post-war life remained largely unspecified, but her development trajectory suggested positive potential.

BLAISE ZABINI

Blaise Zabini was an intelligent Slytherin student whose aloofness and emotional distance from his peers characterized his school experience. His unwillingness to commit to any side during the war or to participate actively in either Death Eater ideology or resistance activity reflected his prioritization of personal survival over principle. His successful avoidance of taking sides allowed him to quietly survive the conflict without moral commitment.

Zabini's neutrality, though perhaps pragmatic, contrasted sharply with those who chose to fight for principle. His post-war survival and his ability to reintegrate into society suggested that such neutrality was possible yet carried its own costs—permanent separation from genuine community and purpose. His character illustrated how choosing not to choose had consequences as real as active commitment.

His strategy of remaining mostly silent and avoiding attention allowed him to maintain distance from direct ideological confrontation. His observant nature and his intelligence suggested genuine capacity for independent thought despite family pressure.

Nott's survival of the war and his ability to avoid direct commitment reflected his strategic withdrawal from active participation in either ideology. His post-war fate remained largely unspecified, suggesting his limited narrative importance despite his capacity for intelligence and observation. Bulstrode's aggressive personality and her commitment to supremacist ideology made her one of Hogwarts' consistent bullies.
